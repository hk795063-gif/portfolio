What I set out to learn

How plant biotechnology research runs end to end: tissue culture, genetic transformation, nucleic acid work, gene-expression analysis and supporting bioinformatics.

What I Did

Did (with peers)

Rice drought-stress phenotyping

Set up hydroponic rice seedlings in Hoagland solution in the glasshouse, supplied nutrients at the two-leaf stage, and applied PEG-6000 osmotic stress at the four-leaf stage. Treatments: control, 10% PEG, 20% PEG, across 12 cultivars (nine commercial, three research lines). Root harvesting and snap-freezing were observed.

Did

In silico primer design

Retrieved a rice gene sequence from NCBI and designed primers in NCBI Primer-BLAST, balancing product size, melting temperature, specificity, GC content and self-complementarity; annotated binding sites in SnapGene. Also designed full-length cloning primers with a 5' GC clamp, a restriction site and a gene-specific sequence. Checked computationally only.

Did (supervised)

Written analysis of gel electrophoresis and PCR parameters

Analyzed how agarose concentration, ladder choice, voltage and run time depend on expected fragment size, common problems such as band smiling and smearing, and the core components of a PCR reaction.

What I Observed and Studied

Observed

TRIzol and silica-column RNA extraction, genomic DNA extraction, agarose gel electrophoresis, PCR running, media preparation with pH calibration and autoclaving, wheat seed sterilization, Agrobacterium screening, and Agrobacterium-mediated CRISPR/Cas9 transformation of potato explants.

Observed

A nanotherapeutic mastitis model demonstrated in the biocontainment laboratory.

Studied

cDNA synthesis and RT-qPCR (reference genes, the comparative threshold method), and how phenotype data connect to GWAS and candidate genes for stress tolerance.

What I learned

Small deviations in pH, RNA handling or primer design change experimental outcomes. Computational primer checks can catch unsuitable candidates before reagents are spent. Techniques that seemed separate in coursework connect into one workflow.

About the Initiative

BioMerchant is a student-led biotechnology initiative based in Islamabad that explores botanical ingredients and biotechnology principles in the development of everyday personal-care products. The project aims to translate academic knowledge about biological materials into practical product concepts, combining botanical ingredient exploration, literature-based research, and basic product evaluation with early-stage entrepreneurship.

My Role — R&D & Formulation

As an R&D Formulation Assistant, my realistic responsibilities included:

  • Conducting literature reviews on potential botanical ingredients.
  • Performing preliminary/student-level physical evaluations.
  • Documenting development processes and assisting with presentation.

Direct Formulation Contributions

Products I directly helped formulate alongside teammates (prototype development stage):

Botanical Sunscreen

Explored botanical ingredients alongside zinc oxide as the intended UV-filtering component. Focused on prototype formulation, refinement, and physical evaluation.

Note: No verified SPF or UV-protection claims are made. Preliminary development only.

Hand & Foot Cream

Developed a botanical formulation prototype. Iterated based on texture and consistency evaluation, physical observations, and sensory refinement.

Focused purely on structural consistency and sensory application profiles.

Beard Oil

Explored a plant-oil based formulation. Focused on ingredient selection, prototype development, and adjusting for sensory and application considerations.

Evaluated for viscosity, absorption rates, and phase stability.

Iterative Workflow

Scientific literature guided early decisions, followed by practical experimentation for prototype feasibility.

Evaluation & Preliminary Stability

Selected prototypes were observed over their development and storage period for basic physical changes. This included monitoring:

  • General appearance and visible phase separation
  • Consistency and structural homogeneity
  • pH levels (where relevant to the formulation)

* Student-level evaluations to guide refinement, not formal stability studies.

Wider Team Contributions

I assisted teammates with their led prototypes, including an anti-acne serum, hydrating gel, face mist, lip gel, and a DEET-free mosquito-repellent. Not all concepts progressed to presentation.

Beyond Formulation

Gained exposure to product communication by contributing to the brand's logo identity, conceptualizing packaging, and assisting in the design of the initiative's digital portal.

Reflection

A formulation that looks promising in literature does not automatically translate into a stable or validated product. My work with BioMerchant demonstrated that product development is highly iterative—requiring continuous physical evaluation and refinement. Moving from a prototype concept to a potential commercial product requires rigorous validation, documentation, and a strong understanding of regulatory considerations.
